A product video almost never goes wrong on set. It goes wrong in the brief, when the brand pictures one film and the production team pictures another. The fix is not a longer brief. It is answering the right few questions before anyone picks up a camera.

## 1. Where will it live?

A website hero film, a Meta ad and a TikTok reel are three different videos. List every place the content will run, with the format for each: 16:9, 1:1, 4:5 or 9:16, and roughly how long.

## 2. What should someone feel, and then do?

Pick one feeling and one action. Calm and premium, then visit the store. Hungry, then order now. Videos that try to say everything usually say nothing.

## 3. What makes this product different?

Not the feature list, the one thing. The grain of the wood, the texture of the cream, the stitching on the shoe. That detail becomes the hero shot.

## 4. Who is it for?

Describe the customer in a sentence or two. A first apartment buyer and a family upgrading their living room need different rooms, styling and pace.

## 5. Show references, and say what you like about them

Three to five links are perfect. Say what you like in each one, the light, the pace, the music, so the team copies the right thing and not the whole video.

## 6. What are the must haves?

- Products, colours and variants that must appear

- Logo, packaging and any legal text

- Music preferences or brand sounds

- Deadline and launch date

- Who approves, and how many revision rounds

## The one page brief

Put those six answers on one page and you have a better brief than most. A good production team will turn it into a treatment and shot list for you to approve before the shoot.
