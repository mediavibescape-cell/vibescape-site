Almost every business that comes to us has pressed Boost Post at some point. It is right there under every post, it takes a minute and it feels like marketing. The problem is not that boosting never works. The problem is that you cannot tell when it does.

## What Boost Post actually does

Boosting takes one existing post and shows it to more people, with a short list of settings. It is built for reach and engagement: likes, comments and views. That is useful for awareness, but likes do not pay rent, and the button gives you very little control over who sees the post or what you want them to do next.

## What a real campaign adds

- **An objective.** Sales, leads or messages, so Meta optimises for the result you actually want.

- **Tracking.** A pixel or conversions API, so you can see which ad brought which order or lead.

- **Testing.** Several creatives at once, so data decides the winner instead of opinion.

- **Audiences.** Retargeting people who visited your site or watched your video, not just a broad interest.

- **Budget control.** Money moves to what works, and losing ads are switched off quickly.

## Creative is the biggest lever

The ad itself decides most of the result. A clear product, a reason to care in the first second and a simple next step will beat clever targeting with a weak video every time. This is why we shoot and edit ad creative in house, and why we make several versions to test instead of one perfect ad.

## Leads need a system after the ad

For lead based businesses the ad is only half the job. If a lead waits two days for a call, the money is already wasted. We connect lead campaigns to a CRM so every lead is assigned, called and tracked, and the monthly report shows what happened after the click.

## When boosting is fine

Boosting a post you want more people to see, like an event or an announcement, is fine. Just treat it as visibility, not as a sales channel.
