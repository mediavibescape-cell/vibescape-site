Most rebrand checklists start on the day you have already decided to rebrand. Audit, strategy, logo, guidelines, launch. Useful, but they skip the harder question: do you actually need one?

A rebrand is expensive in the ways that do not show up on an invoice. Months of internal debate, every touchpoint redone, and customers who have to learn to recognise you again. Done for the wrong reason, it throws away recognition you spent years building. Done too late, it means you have been leaving money on the table with a look that no longer matches the business.

So this rebrand checklist works in two parts. First, 12 signs your brand has outgrown its look. Then, what to do once you have ticked enough of them.

## Part one: 12 signs your brand has outgrown its look

Go through these honestly. One or two ticks usually means a refresh. Five or more, spread across different groups, usually means the brand needs real work.

### The business changed, the brand did not

- **1. You sell something different now.** The brand was built for your first product or your first city. The business has moved on and the identity is still describing the old version.

- **2. Your price went up and your look stayed put.** You moved upmarket but the packaging, website and social still look like the budget option. Buyers judge price by presentation before they read a word.

- **3. You are entering a new market.** A name, colour or line that works in Lahore may read very differently in Dubai, London or New York. If you now sell abroad, check the brand still travels.

- **4. You merged, split or added a sub brand.** Two logos side by side is not a brand architecture. If customers cannot tell how your products relate, the system needs a rethink.

### The brand is hard to use

- **5. Every designer interprets it differently.** If your agency, your printer and your social freelancer all produce something that looks like a different company, there is no system, just a logo.

- **6. The logo breaks at small sizes.** It looks fine on a sign and turns into a smudge as a profile picture, a favicon or a tiny mark on a pack. Most people now meet your brand on a phone first.

- **7. It has no motion.** Reels, ads and product films need a brand that moves. If nobody has ever decided how your logo animates, how text enters or what your transitions feel like, every video invents its own answer.

- **8. Nobody can find the files.** No guidelines, no master files, fonts nobody has a licence for. This is a sign that the brand was a one off delivery, not a system your team can run.

### Customers and competitors are telling you something

- **9. People describe you wrongly.** Ask five customers what you do and who you are for. If the answers are vague or wrong, the brand is not doing its job of explaining you.

- **10. You look like everyone in your category.** Same fonts, same beige, same lifestyle photography. If you cover your logo and the ad could belong to any competitor, you are paying for attention you are not keeping. We wrote about this trap in [Premium bola tha. Beige nahi.](/journal/premium-bola-tha-beige-nahi)

- **11. The team is embarrassed by it.** When your sales team apologises for the deck or your staff stop sharing company posts, take it seriously. Internal pride is an early signal of external perception.

- **12. Ads and pages underperform for no clear reason.** If targeting and offer look right but creative keeps falling flat, the brand may be the bottleneck. This one needs care: test creative first before blaming the identity.

## Refresh or rebrand?

Not every tick means start over. The difference matters because a refresh keeps the recognition you have earned, while a full rebrand resets it.

- **Refresh** when the strategy still fits but the execution has aged or drifted. Tighten the logo, fix the type and colour system, add motion rules, write guidelines. Signs 5 to 8 usually point here.

- **Rebrand** when the business itself has changed: who you sell to, what you sell, where, or at what price. Signs 1 to 4 and 9 to 10 usually point here, because no amount of polish fixes a brand that describes the wrong company.

A rule of thumb: if you can still say what the brand stands for in one sentence and your customers agree, refresh. If you cannot, the problem is strategy, and the logo is the last thing to touch. We made the same point in [Logo badalna rebrand nahi hota](/journal/logo-badalna-rebrand-nahi).

## Part two: the rebrand checklist itself

Once you have decided, this is the order that avoids the most rework.

### Before any design

- **Write down why.** One paragraph on what is changing in the business and what the new brand has to do. If you cannot write it, you are not ready.

- **Decide what you keep.** List the assets with real recognition: a colour, a shape, a name, a jingle, a pack format. Keeping one or two of these lets loyal customers follow you across.

- **Talk to customers.** Even a handful of short conversations will tell you more than a week of internal workshops.

- **Look at the category.** Collect what competitors look like in one place. The goal is to find the space nobody owns, not to copy the leader.

- **Check the name.** If the name is changing, check trademarks and domains in every market you sell in before anyone falls in love with an option. Get proper legal advice for this part.

### Strategy and identity

- **Positioning in one line.** Who you are for, what you do, and why you over the alternatives.

- **Voice.** How you sound in English, Urdu or both, with real examples of headlines, captions and replies.

- **Identity system.** Logo, type, colour, layout rules, photography direction and motion. Test it on a pack, a reel, a story, a billboard and a website before you sign off.

- **Guidelines.** Short enough that people actually read them, with master files and templates attached.

### Rollout

- **List every touchpoint.** Website, social profiles, Google Business Profile, packaging, signage, invoices, email signatures, ads, uniforms. It is always longer than you think.

- **Protect your search traffic.** If URLs or the domain change, plan redirects from every old page to its new home and update Search Console. A rebrand that loses your rankings is a very expensive way to look nicer.

- **Brief the team first.** Staff should hear about it and see the new look before customers do, and know how to explain the change.

- **Launch with a story.** Tell customers what changed and why, especially if they will notice a new name or pack on the shelf.

- **Review after launch.** Check that people are using the system properly a month in, and fix the templates that cause the most mistakes.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting with the logo.** It is the most visible part and the least strategic. Start with the line and the system.

- **Designing by committee.** Collect input widely, decide narrowly. One owner signs off.

- **Rebranding out of boredom.** Your team sees the brand every day. Customers see it for a few seconds. Being tired of it is not the same as it not working.

- **Forgetting video.** An identity that only works on paper will be reinvented badly in every reel.
