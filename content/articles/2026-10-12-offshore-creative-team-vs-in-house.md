Every growing brand and agency hits the same wall. There is more creative work than people, and hiring takes months. The two usual options are hiring in house or working with an outside team, often in another country. Both can work. Both can go badly. The difference is mostly in how you set them up.

## When in house makes sense

An in house team is worth it when the work is constant, deeply tied to your product and needs people in the room. If you publish every day and the brand changes weekly, a person who sits with your product team is hard to beat.

## When an outside team makes sense

- The volume goes up and down, so full time salaries sit idle in quiet months.

- You need several skills, like editing, motion, design and web, but not a full time person for each.

- You need production, such as a studio, crew and post, that is expensive to own.

- You want to start next week, not after a three month hiring process.

## What makes offshore teams fail

Most bad experiences come from the same few things: vague briefs, no single point of contact, work disappearing into email threads, and nobody owning quality. Cost savings do not matter if you spend the saved money on revisions.

## What makes them work

- **One owner.** A named person responsible for your account and the final quality.

- **One place for everything.** Briefs, files, comments and approvals in a shared portal, not scattered across chats.

- **Overlap hours.** Enough shared working time for a daily check in when it matters.

- **A paid test.** One real project before committing to a retainer.

- **Clear scope.** Revision rounds and turnaround agreed upfront.

## The real comparison

Compare the full cost, not the day rate: salaries, software, equipment, management time and the cost of a bad hire on one side, and a monthly fee with output you can measure on the other. For many brands and agencies the right answer is a mix: a small in house lead with an outside team doing the production.
